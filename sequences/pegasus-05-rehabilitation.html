Episode II — Pegasus, Case V

Sanctioned in 2021. Bought by American investors in 2025. Nothing else changed.

5W+1H — Case Summary

WHO: NSO Group — the Israeli spyware maker behind Pegasus — and the chain of owners, lobbyists, and political appointees who tried to rescue it: from its three founders to US private equity, a Hollywood producer, and a former US ambassador.TC

WHAT: A multi-year rehabilitation effort: getting off the US Entity List, surviving lawsuits, and rebranding under American ownership — while the underlying product and its documented abuses continued.BISTC

WHEN: November 2021 — Entity List. May 2025 — a $167M+ US jury verdict against NSO. October 2025 — acquisition by US investors. November 2025 — a former US ambassador as chairman. June 2026 — a contempt motion for fresh attacks. July 2026 — a co-founder's diplomatic passport exposed.BISAICLOCCRP

WHERE: Israel (HQ), Washington DC (sanctions, lobbying, courts), and Panama (where NSO's co-founder traveled on a diplomatic passport in 2013).OCCRP

WHY: Sanctions and lawsuits threatened the business itself. Rehabilitation was the survival strategy — new ownership, new faces, and the promise of change, aimed at reopening doors to the largest market of all.TC

HOW: Lobbying firms tied to the administration, ownership transfers that shed debt, and politically connected appointments — executed while court filings and forensic reports kept documenting the same behavior.TCCL

The sanction

In November 2021, the US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security added NSO Group to the Entity List — alongside Candiru, a fellow Israeli spyware maker. The stated reason was direct: NSO had "developed and supplied spyware to foreign governments that used these tools to maliciously target government officials, journalists, businesspeople, activists, academics, and embassy workers."BIS

The listing carried real teeth. American companies could no longer sell to NSO without a license, and the review policy was a presumption of denial. For a company whose survival depended on US technology, the measure was designed to be painful.BIS

The lawsuit that would not go away

NSO was fighting on a second front. In October 2019, WhatsApp and its parent company Meta had sued NSO in a US federal court in California, alleging the company had used WhatsApp's servers to target more than 1,400 users' devices with Pegasus. The case would drag on for years.AI

In December 2024, a judge ruled NSO Group liable for having unlawfully targeted WhatsApp's users and infrastructure. The following May, a US jury ordered NSO to pay more than $167 million in damages. Amnesty International called the verdict a "momentous win in the fight against spyware abuse."AI

The litigation also produced an uncomfortable fact for the company's own narrative. Legal documents revealed, for the first time, that NSO itself — not its government customers — was the entity that "installs and extracts" the data from targeted devices. The "we only sell the tool" defense was, on the record, incomplete.AI

The lobbying push

Sanctions had to be lifted, and that required Washington. NSO Group spent years trying to get off the US blacklist — as recently as May 2025, with the help of a lobbying firm tied to the Trump administration. The effort was reported by The Washington Post and WIRED, both describing a deliberate push to win over the administration that now controlled the decision.TC

The lobbying track ran in parallel with a more structural one: changing who owned the company.TC

The ownership shuffle

NSO Group's ownership had never been stable. Founded by Niv Karmi, Shalev Hulio, and Omri Lavie, it was acquired by the US private equity firm Francisco Partners in 2014. Lavie and Hulio took control back in 2019 with help from the European firm Novalpina. In 2021, management of the fund was taken over by Berkeley Research Group. In 2023, Lavie emerged again as majority owner.TC

In October 2025, the cycle turned again. NSO confirmed to TechCrunch that a group of American investors — led, according to Calcalist, by Hollywood producer Robert Simonds — had taken controlling ownership. The deal was worth tens of millions of dollars, and the restructuring shed roughly $600 million in debt. All three co-founders, including Lavie, lost their shares.TC

The company's spokesperson insisted nothing had really moved: "This investment does not mean that the company is moving out of Israeli regulatory or operational control." Headquarters and core operations, he said, remain in Israel, supervised by the Israeli Ministry of Defense.TC

The new face

A month later, in November 2025, NSO announced a new chairman: David Friedman, the former US Ambassador to Israel under the Trump administration. NSO filings stated that "it is reasonably foreseeable that a law enforcement or intelligence agency of the United States will use Pegasus." The company was positioning itself as an American partner.TOI

For researchers who had tracked NSO for years, the acquisition was a warning, not a reset. Citizen Lab's John Scott-Railton, who has documented Pegasus abuse for a decade, put it bluntly: "NSO is a company with a long history of going against American interests and supporting the hacking of American officials. In what world can such a person be trusted to properly oversee a company like NSO Group?"TC

The behavior did not change

In June 2026, Meta's WhatsApp said it would ask a US court to hold NSO Group in contempt, accusing the company of once again using WhatsApp to try to lure targets into downloading Pegasus — a direct violation of the court order from the case NSO had just lost. Citizen Lab's Scott-Railton observed: "NSO Group is behaving just like the dictators they service — treating US courts as something to bypass, and hope that nobody notices."CL

The state behind the company

In July 2026, OCCRP and partners published records showing that NSO co-founder Shalev Hulio traveled to Panama in December 2013 on an Israeli diplomatic passport, telling Panamanian immigration he would be staying at the Israeli embassy. NSO had sold Pegasus to Panama the year before, with Hulio signing the contract. He denied the finding; the Israeli ministries did not respond.OCCRP

"The separation between the government and NSO is a smokescreen." — Eitay Mack, Israeli lawyer and human rights activist, on NSO's claimed independence from the Israeli stateOCCRP

The diplomatic passport finding did not change NSO's ownership. But it answered a question the rehabilitation tour had carefully avoided: a company that sold spyware to autocratic governments had long claimed it operated independently of its own government. The records suggested otherwise.OCCRPCL

Put the pieces together and the strategy is visible. Sanctioned, NSO lobbied. Sued, NSO kept attacking. Unmasked, NSO found new owners. The ownership changed; the product did not. As of mid-2026, the company remains on the US Entity List, its blacklisting intact — and its behavior, according to the court filings, unchanged.CLTC

This is what rehabilitation looks like when it is aimed at the market instead of the behavior.TC
