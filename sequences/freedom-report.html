Episode I — State of Digital Freedom

The ship has already hit the iceberg

On June 14, 2026, Pavel Durov stood on the Oslo Freedom Forum stage and said something most people in the room didn't want to hear: "Our ship has already hit the iceberg." Not "might hit." Not "is approaching." Already hit.02:29

He wasn't talking about climate change or geopolitics. He was talking about personal freedom — the kind most people in Western democracies assumed was permanent, inherited, unassailable. The kind that erodes one law, one precedent, one emergency measure at a time, until one day you look up and realize it's gone.

This sequence uses Durov's speech as a hook — not as its authority. The speech raises the questions. The evidence that follows stands on its own sourcing.

The pretext is always children

Durov's most pointed observation wasn't about authoritarian regimes. It was about the West. "They used protection of children as a pretext — in fact, they wanted more control over political speech."09:09 That framing is Durov's rhetoric, and it stays attributed to him. The underlying policy disputes are a matter of public record.

He was referring to the UK's Online Safety Act — legislation that received royal assent on October 26, 2023, and whose illegal-content duties for large platforms became enforceable in March 2025.OSA 2023 In his speech, Durov cited what he described as a UK government submission in a High Court case, quoting it as saying "the principal part of the Online Safety Act had nothing to do with protecting children" and was instead "aimed at capturing large platforms with significant influence over public discourse." The quote is reproduced as Durov presented it; whether the act lives up to that characterization is contested, and the act itself is public law readers can inspect.09:09

He applied the same lens to Brussels. The European Commission has pursued what critics call "Chat Control" since 2022 — formally, the proposed Regulation to Prevent and Combat Child Sexual Abuse.COM 2022/209EP The official justification: child protection. The contested mechanism: scanning. The most expansive versions would have required messaging apps to run detection software on users' devices, inspecting messages, photos, and videos before they are encrypted — client-side scanning, which critics describe as undermining end-to-end encryption without formally "breaking" it. The proposal is not law. The Council dropped mandatory detection orders in November 2025, and trilogue negotiations continue with detection still unresolved — as of this review, no scanning mandate has been enacted.EP

"Those who are willing to give up their essential liberty for some temporary safety deserve neither liberty nor safety." — Benjamin Franklin, echoed by Durov at Oslo13:14

The enforcement trap

What Durov called "selective enforcement of laws" is the mechanism he described at greatest length. The pattern, as he told it: flood an industry with contradictory regulations so compliance becomes impossible, then choose who to persecute based on political loyalty. Those who cooperate get ignored. Those who resist get hit with the full weight of the law. This is his account — a description of the speech, not a proven system.18:14

He cited his own experience: criminal investigations in Russia, what he described as a secret deal offered by French intelligence during his 2024 detention in Paris, and what he called the European Commission's alleged offer to Elon Musk — censor inconvenient narratives or face fines. According to Durov, Musk refused and his company was later fined €120 million on "seemingly unrelated issues." All of this is Durov's account of events, delivered from a stage, and is reproduced here as allegation and rhetoric — not as established fact.20:36

Durov also claimed the US Congress has published emails documenting European Commission pressure on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube to silence political narratives during the pandemic — narratives that, he said, turned out to be "at least partially true, and in any case worthy of public debate." That is his characterization of congressional material, presented as such.20:36

There is no second West

Durov closed with a warning that most people missed: "There is no second West. There is no backup civilization."29:03 For decades, dissidents from authoritarian countries could flee west. But if the current trajectory continues, he said, "they will have a hard time trying to understand whether they have already left their repressive homeland or they're just entering another open-air prison."28:30

His projection of the AI age was blunt: "Every message will be monitored. Every thought scored. Every relationship mapped."27:34 As Durov put it, the KGB had finite resources. AI does not. That is his argument — not a measured statement of current capability.

He returned to the ship. The passengers of the Titanic, he said, didn't want to leave the ship for almost two hours after it hit the iceberg. By the time they panicked, it was too late.01:48

"The only way forward is to fix this ship."29:43
