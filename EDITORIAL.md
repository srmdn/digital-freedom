# Editorial Methodology

Digital Freedom documents important events in digital privacy, surveillance, censorship, encryption, information freedom, and related technologies — through interactive narratives that read like terminal sessions or intelligence dossiers.

The presentation is cinematic. The sourcing is not.

## Mission

Digital Freedom maintains a long-term archive of digital freedom stories. The project's principle is short:

> **Cinematic presentation. Forensic discipline.**

Stories are told in unconventional formats — terminal playback, classified-file interfaces, dossier reconstructions — but every material claim behind the format is sourced from public records.

## Sourcing

Prefer, in roughly this order:

1. Court documents and legal proceedings
2. Legislation and government records
3. Official security advisories
4. Technical research institutions (Citizen Lab, Google Project Zero, Amnesty Security Lab, and comparable labs)
5. Forensic reports
6. Reputable investigative journalism
7. Reliable secondary reporting

Primary sources are preferred when available. Social media posts, aggregators, and viral claims are not acceptable as sole sources.

## Evidence classes

Every material claim belongs to one of these classes:

| Class | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **FACT** | Directly established by reliable public evidence. |
| **FORENSIC FINDING** | Established through technical analysis, device forensics, or comparable technical investigation. |
| **ALLEGATION** | A claim made by an identified party, not necessarily independently established. |
| **ATTRIBUTION** | An assessment linking an activity to an operator, organization, or government — always naming who made the assessment. |
| **EXPERT INTERPRETATION** | Interpretation from an identified researcher, lawyer, NGO, journalist, or other expert. |
| **EDITORIAL INFERENCE** | A conclusion Digital Freedom derives from established evidence — allowed, and always distinguishable from directly established fact. |

Sequences distinguish these states rather than collapsing them. A phone number **selected** in a surveillance queue is not a **confirmed infection**. A **proposed** law is not an **enacted** one. A **jury verdict** is not a **final judgment**. An **allegation** is not an **established fact**.

## Language discipline

| Not the same as | Meaning |
|-----------------|---------|
| targeted | confirmed compromised |
| selected | infected |
| alleged | established |
| proposed | enacted |
| attributed | proven |
| jury verdict | final judgment |
| no visible indication to the target | no forensic trace |

Some rhetorical statements — for example, *"The pretext is always children"* — are attributed to their speakers (in this case Pavel Durov) and are not presented as Digital Freedom's own universal empirical conclusion.

## Editorial voice

Digital Freedom does not pretend to have no editorial perspective. It interprets evidence, draws comparisons, and argues.

The line is this: **editorial conclusions never masquerade as sourced factual claims.** Facts, quotations, dates, technical findings, and legal claims carry source markers. Interpretive passages are written so a careful reader can tell them apart — and are grounded in cited evidence, never invented.

## Simulated interfaces

Terminal hooks, classified-file layouts, and intelligence-dossier aesthetics are **editorial presentation devices**. They are not leaked government documents. The underlying events and evidence are sourced from public records, and sequences carry a note saying so. The project does not publish classified material.

## Data practices

When a sequence compares datasets (for example Freedom House, V-Dem, and Reporters Without Borders), each dataset is identified by:

- report name
- edition / year
- what it measures
- score direction
- access / review date

Indices that measure different things on different scales are never presented as normalized equivalents.

## Corrections

Material factual errors are corrected transparently, in place, with the correction noted where the reader can find it.

For significant historical corrections, the project records:

1. Date of correction
2. Sequence and claim affected
3. What previously appeared
4. What was changed and why (source)

Git history serves as part of the audit trail: corrections are committed as distinct, discoverable changes rather than silently rewritten.

To report an error: open an issue at [github.com/srmdn/digital-freedom/issues](https://github.com/srmdn/digital-freedom/issues) with the claim, the source that contradicts it, and a link to the relevant sequence.
